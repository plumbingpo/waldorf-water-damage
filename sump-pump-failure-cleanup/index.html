Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Waldorf: What to Know

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. For the job, this means soaked porous items such as drywall and carpet pad are usually discarded, and what stays gets washed and disinfected first, then dried.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Our Process

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Low spots, the area by the pit and the rest of the floor are cleared of water and silt.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Carpet and Flooring

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Stored Belongings

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.
