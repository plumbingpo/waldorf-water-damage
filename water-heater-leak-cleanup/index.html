Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Waldorf: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

Our Waldorf team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Platform and Subfloor

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Stored Belongings

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.
