How Mold Affects Health

Mold produces allergens, irritants, and in some cases toxic substances called mycotoxins. You can take them in by breathing, by touching them, or by swallowing them. The severity of health effects depends on the type and concentration of mold, duration of exposure, and individual sensitivity. Typical symptoms are coughing, wheezing, a congested nose, irritated eyes and throat, headaches, and skin rashes. For those with a compromised immune system, asthma, or allergies, the reaction to mold may be worse.

Who Is Most at Risk

While anyone can be affected by mold exposure, certain groups face higher risk: infants and young children, elderly individuals, people with respiratory conditions such as asthma or COPD, individuals with allergies or immune system disorders, and people taking immunosuppressive medications. If someone this vulnerable is exposed, prompt remediation becomes especially important.
