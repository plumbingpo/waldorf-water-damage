Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Waldorf

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

Our Waldorf crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Our Process

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Pull and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Extract the Water

We extract standing water out of carpet and off hard floors. If it is trapped beneath a floating floor or the cabinets, toe kicks come off or boards are lifted so we can get to it.

3

Dry in Place

We set air movers and dehumidifiers and, where possible, dry cabinets and hardwood in place with directed airflow so they do not have to be torn out.

4

Sanitize and Take Final Readings

Any surface that dishwasher or washer discharge reached is treated with an antimicrobial. We take final moisture readings before the appliance goes back so it is not set on a damp floor.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • Washer supply hoses made of rubber that get weak over the years and burst from the pressure.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.

Wall Bases and Trim

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Rooms Next Door

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.

  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
  • If it was gray discharge, which must be sanitized, or clean water from the supply.
