Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Waldorf Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
