Kitchen Water Damage Help in Waldorf

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. Our Waldorf crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

What It Looks Like

  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Sink Drain Leaks

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.

Flooring Over Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.
